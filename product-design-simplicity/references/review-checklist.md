# Product Design Simplicity Review Checklist

Use this checklist for product critiques, UX audits, flow reviews, design-system reviews, and feature simplification.

## 1. User And Job

- Who is the primary user?
- What job are they trying to complete?
- What does success look like?
- What context matters: urgency, expertise, device, frequency, risk, collaboration, or compliance?
- What assumptions are missing?

## 2. Primary Path

- Is the main task visible without explanation?
- Is the first useful action obvious?
- Are the steps in the order users naturally expect?
- Are rare or advanced paths competing with the common path?
- Can the user complete the task without reading instructional copy?

## 3. Information Hierarchy

- Is the most important information visually dominant?
- Are headings, labels, and grouping doing real work?
- Is similar information grouped and dissimilar information separated?
- Are repeated concepts named consistently?
- Is any section present because the organization thinks in that structure rather than the user?

## 4. Choice And Decision Load

- Are users being asked to decide too early?
- Are choices meaningfully different?
- Are defaults safe, useful, and inspectable?
- Can invalid or irrelevant options be removed in context?
- Are users forced to configure before they understand value?

## 5. State, Feedback, And Recovery

- Is the current state clear?
- Does every important action produce visible feedback?
- Are loading, saving, syncing, errors, and success states specific?
- Can users undo, edit, cancel, retry, or recover from mistakes?
- Are destructive or consequential actions clear before commitment?

## 6. Language

- Are labels written in the user's vocabulary?
- Are button verbs specific?
- Is helper text replacing a design fix?
- Are error messages actionable and calm?
- Are empty states useful without becoming marketing copy?

## 7. Accessibility And Inclusion

- Does simplification preserve keyboard access, focus order, visible focus, labels, contrast, and screen reader clarity?
- Does it avoid relying only on color, position, memory, or hover?
- Are touch targets, text sizes, and spacing appropriate for the context?
- Are users with different expertise levels supported without separate confusing modes?

## 8. Trust And Control

- Is the system transparent about risk, cost, privacy, automation, and irreversible effects?
- Can users inspect and change important defaults?
- Are automated choices explainable when they matter?
- Does the product avoid hiding information to make the interface seem simpler?

## 9. Recommendation Filter

Before proposing a change, check:

- Does this remove a real user burden?
- Is it the smallest effective change?
- What user capability might be lost?
- What must remain visible, reversible, accessible, or inspectable?
- How would success be verified?
